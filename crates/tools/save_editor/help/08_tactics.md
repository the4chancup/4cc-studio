The bottom of the team view is the **Tactics** part, the team's game plan: which of the
three presets is on screen, its formation settings, the advanced instructions
and the team's automatic settings. A field the save's version does not hold
stays in its place, disabled, with a note naming the first version that has it.

The part is a grid of three columns and three rows: the preset bar level with
the formation bar at the top; **Formation settings**, the pitch and **Player
assignments** in the middle; **Advanced instructions** and **Automatic
settings** beside the two player lists at the bottom.

The **pitch** is where the formation takes shape, drawn like the game's own
formation screen: each starter stands at his spot, drawn as his portrait
over a badge with his position and his name below it (a name too long for
the badge scrolls now and then, as in the game; the **Scroll names**
checkbox at the right of the formation bar holds them still when off, and
the setting is remembered). The badge's right end shows his overall at the
position he plays there, as the game's does, and a gold, silver or bronze
outline round the badge names his medal tier under the active ruleset,
where he has one. The portraits come from
the game picked at the top of the sidebar, out of the `download` folder
inside the **PES folder** setting. When that folder is missing, or a file
it lists is missing, the starters show the game's blank portrait instead.
Dragging a pitch player to a new place moves him there, snapped to the
positions the save can hold, and gives him the position the game's own
editor would there (which can depend on his ratings: the spot offers up
to two positions and he takes the one he rates higher at). While you
hold him, he moves one step at a time and the point you grabbed stays
under the pointer. The pitch stays lit only where he is rated A or B,
and his badge shows the position he would take if you let go there, so
it changes as he crosses from one area into another. Nothing changes
until you let go: bring him back to where he started and he keeps his
position. Dropping him on another starter (the goalkeeper excepted)
trades their places and positions in the formation on screen alone, as
the game's own drag does: the preset's other two formations and the other
presets keep theirs. While you hold him over the other, that one's marker
is marked with corner brackets and the dragged one turns half transparent,
so a drag meant only to move him can still be steered aside onto the
grass. A bench player
dropped on a pitch player swaps them, and a pitch player dropped on a
bench row does the same. Clicking a pitch player opens
his details beside his marker: a position pick (it offers only the
positions his spot allows, as the game's Select Position menu does), the
exact X and Y fields (moving him with them changes his position as a
drag there would) and **Make GK**,
which puts him in goal and brings the goalkeeper out onto his spot. The
details stay where they opened while X and Y move the player, so the
fields' arrows stay under the pointer, and a click anywhere outside
closes them. The
goalkeeper cannot be dragged and his position and spot stay fixed.
The **Kick-off**, **In-possession** and **Out-of-possession** buttons on the
formation bar pick which of the preset's three formations it shows. The game
uses the second and third only while the preset's **Fluid formation** is on,
so while it is off (and always on PES 2015) the buttons stay on Kick-off and
cannot be clicked. The formation bar's Copy/swap still works, so you can
prepare the other two before switching it on. Switching formations changes
nothing by itself. The formation's name in the
pitch's bottom-left corner (such as 3-4-2-1) counts the players in each line
by their positions, back to front, as the game's formation screen does, so
it changes when a player's position does but not when he only moves.

The two lists under the pitch set who plays and in which order: the starting
eleven on the left, the bench on the right;
the bench lists the players alone, never an empty slot. Drag a row onto another to move or swap it. A starter's row dropped on
another starter's row, or on a pitch player, swaps the two players everywhere,
in every formation of every preset, as picking one and then the other does in
the game. A pitch player dropped on a starter's row does the same. A bench player dropped on a starter takes
his place and every job he held (set pieces, joining the attack, instruction
targets). The captain stays who he was.

The **Player assignments** rows set who takes each set piece, the captain and
who joins the attack: each is a label over a chip naming its holder, or
**Unassigned** where nobody holds it. **Join the attack** has three chips,
one per joining player. Click a chip, then click the pitch player. Join
keeps its mode open so up to three can be picked or unpicked, and all three
chips stay highlighted while it is open; click a chip again to close it.

The **Preset** buttons on the left of the preset bar pick which of the team's
three presets the panels below edit. Switching presets changes nothing by
itself. The **Fluid formation** checkbox beside them (PES 2016 and later)
decides whether the game uses the second and third formations.

**Formation settings** pairs each attacking setting with its defensive
counterpart in six rows: attacking style beside defensive style, buildup
beside containment area, attacking zone beside pressure, positioning beside
defensive line, support range beside compactness, and numbers in attack
beside numbers in defence. Each is a label over its control: a slider with
its value at the right, three choices (Few, Medium, Many) for the numbers,
or a two-choice switch named as the game names its two choices. The
defensive style's choices are shortened so the row fits; point at one to
read its full name.

**Advanced instructions** (PES 2017 and later) sets the team's two attack and
two defence instructions: the attack column lists the attacking choices and
the defence column the defending ones. Tight Marking is never offered, because
it marks an opponent and is set during a match, not here. A save already
holding it keeps it, and the row still names it. Under each instruction a chip
names its target player, or reads "Unassigned". The chip is enabled only for
the instructions that take one of the team's own players (Counter Target and
Defensive): click it, then click the pitch player, as the player assignments
pick theirs. Under an instruction that takes no target the chip stays disabled
and reads "No target", or "An opponent" under a held Tight Marking, whose
target is one of theirs. Picking an instruction that takes no target clears
the pick.

**Automatic settings** holds the automatic substitution setting, a choice of
the game's own names (Off, Very late or Flexible; Very early too on PES 2017
and PES 2021), the offside trap, the preset change and (PES 2017 and later)
the attack/defence levels.

Each bar's **Copy/swap** menu acts on the shown preset or formation: a copy
to, then a swap with, each of the other two (Copy to 2, Copy to 3, Swap with 2
and Swap with 3 from preset 1; Copy to In-possession and so on for a
formation). A preset copy or swap moves the whole preset, man marking
included, and a formation copy or swap moves its eleven slots; the starting
eleven is never touched. Every change, like every edit in the editor, is one
undoable step (Ctrl+Z).
